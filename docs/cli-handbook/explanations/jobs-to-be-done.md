# What makes something a job to be done?

A job is what a performer seeks to accomplish or change in particular circumstances, whichever solution they use. Someone who promised a guest a working sink might call a plumber, arrange a visit by text or ask a housemate to take over. Finishing the call still leaves them responsible. A working sink or an agreed transfer of responsibility settles the commitment. [Worked To-do job](../../../examples/todo/specs/jobs/personal-commitments.job.spec.md#job).

## Choose a useful perspective

**How does the performer judge success across different solutions?** For requirements discovery, the handbook starts with the functional job in Anthony Ulwick's *Outcome-Driven Innovation* (ODI). Mapping its steps gives researchers places to look for desired outcomes. For someone returning to the repair errand, one criterion is the time it takes to identify the next outstanding action. ODI research asks performers how important that criterion is and how satisfied they are with current approaches. Keep those ratings with the dated study so readers know whose judgment they represent. See *Jobs to Be Done: Theory to Practice* (2016, revised 2023), chs. 2 and 4. [Ulwick on customer needs](https://strategyn.com/customer-needs-through-a-jobs-to-be-done-lens/).

**What progress explains a particular choice?** Clayton Christensen's theory asks why an approach fits someone's circumstances. The same person may use a notebook for private errands and exchange texts with a guest about the repair. The texts might help coordinate access (functional progress), ease worry (emotional progress), or show the guest they can count on the person (social progress). Their account can reveal which of these mattered. [Christensen Institute on Jobs to Be Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/).

The questions can inform each other. If one person switched tools because they lost track of who owed the next action, a researcher has a reason to ask other performers how they monitor a commitment. The switch explains one choice. Its frequency and importance across performers remain open. [Jim Kalbach on functional jobs and aspirations](https://experiencinginformation.com/2020/10/23/drills-and-milkshakes-say-yes-to-the-mess/).

## The universal job map

Bettencourt and Ulwick describe eight kinds of accomplishment that can reveal work hidden on either side of an obvious activity. For the person arranging the repair, even a successful call leaves several questions open. [Bettencourt and Ulwick, *The Customer-Centered Innovation Map*](https://hbr.org/2008/05/the-customer-centered-innovation-map) · [Strategyn, *Build Your Job Map*](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/jobs-to-be-done-playbook/build-your-job-map/).

| Universal step | What it reveals in the repair errand |
| --- | --- |
| Define | What has the person promised the guest, and by when? |
| Locate | Where is the plumber’s reply about what they need? |
| Prepare | Which details need assembling so the plumber can plan a visit? |
| Confirm | Can someone let the plumber in at the proposed time? |
| Execute | What will secure the visit? |
| Monitor | How will the person know the reply is overdue? |
| Modify | What changes when the plumber asks for a photo? |
| Conclude | Is the sink working, or has someone else accepted responsibility? |

A map names the accomplishments in the performer's terms, such as “Check what remains.” Someone may check readiness several times or finish without changing the plan. In dotspec a map can be partial, and each step's stage label is optional. See Jim Kalbach, *The Jobs to Be Done Playbook* (2020), ch. 3. [Steps in the To-do job](../../../examples/todo/specs/jobs/personal-commitments.job.spec.md#steps) · [Step and stage syntax](../reference/formats/job.md#steps-stages-and-desired-outcomes).

## Understand a switch

Bob Moesta and the Re-Wired Group use a switch interview to investigate **“Why change now?”** A timeline ties a person’s first doubts, alternatives and eventual choice to events they recall. If a buyer chose for someone else, the interview explains the buyer’s decision. See Moesta, *Learning to Build*, ch. 3. [Re-Wired Group on switch interviews](https://therewiredgroup.com/learn/complete-guide-jobs-to-be-done/) · [The buyer’s timeline](https://therewiredgroup.com/learn/the-six-stages-of-the-buyers-timeline/).

Suppose someone moved their errands from messages to a notebook. Losing an agreement in a long thread could supply the **push** to change. Seeing all the errands together could be the **pull**. Worry about leaving the notebook at home could create **anxiety**, while replying in the original thread was already a **habit**. Asking about the actual decision can reveal which of these forces mattered. Keep that timeline with the evidence for the choice.

A **job story** expresses a circumstance, motivation and intended progress in one sentence. “When my guest asks for an update after a few days, I want to recover what we agreed, so I can give them an answer they can rely on.” A story can carry an interview finding or a hypothesis into design discussion. Its evidence tells the reader which. [Paul Adams on job stories](https://www.intercom.com/blog/accidentally-invented-job-stories) · [Job-story preset](../reference/formats/job.md#checks-and-incomplete-work).

## From a job map to a user needs map

A job map describes what the performer does. Richard Allen's *User Needs Mapping* follows what the performer depends on to meet a need. A broad job can anchor a map of the whole service. A desired outcome lets a team investigate a particular difficulty and trace it to supporting capabilities, their providers and the teams involved. See *User Needs Mapping* (1st ed., 2025), ch. 4, steps 3–7. [Allen's worked moviegoer map](https://www.userneedsmapping.com/moviegoer-walkthrough).

Allen uses short labels on the map. In dotspec, the label **Less reconstruction** links back to the full desired outcome, including the time cost and the circumstance of returning after a break. That gives a team a way to judge a proposed capability. Keeping an entry available tomorrow helps only if the person recorded enough to recover the next action.
